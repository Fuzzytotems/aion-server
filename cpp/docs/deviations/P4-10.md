## M5b-3 client session leak S-1 (2026-09-24/25): what keeps a removed npc, and what the port may release

The leak census of that session (m5b3-client-session.md S-1) warned at 20:15:13 that an Npc (object id 25582) was still alive 10 minutes after
`World.removeObject`, at refcount 1 with no pinning task, and the shutdown hook reported it again with no player online. It left the world at
about 20:05:13 while the Mage hunted striped kerubs and juvenile sparkies around x 1007-1226, y 962-1222 of Poeta (210010000); nothing was
logged at that moment, which fits a kill with no drop whose corpse decays 2 s later.

**What is known about 25582.** Its class, removal time and place, and that exactly one reference outlived every task and every player. Not its
npc id or the holder: the census logs class, object id and refcount only. Two diagnosis passes narrowed it down:

- Ruled out, by 22 lifetime tests (`tests/handlers_ai_core/NpcCastDeathLifetimeTest.cpp`, `tests/effects_mz/NpcEffectLifetimeTest.cpp`): the
  skill, effect, task and player paths of a kill.
- Ruled out as the cause, by the review of the first change: the zone and region races of the `MapRegion.revalidateZones` and
  `World.removeObject` rows below. Every spot of the hunting
  area lies inside AKARIOS_PLAINS, which all cells touched there list, so a region crossing cannot leave a zone entry there that no later
  revalidation or despawn leaves again, and a corpse does not move while it decays. Those two rows are **hardening plus a diagnostic**, not the
  fix of S-1.
- Reproduced (`tests/world/WalkerGroupLifetimeTest.cpp`): the walker formations. Three pool-2 walker routes start in the hunting area:
  F912DD8B and 6C27EAF1 (a juvenile sparkie 210115 and a striped kerub 210133 sharing a spot: a `WalkerGroup`) and 3805A343 (one juvenile
  sparkie at 1134.95/1012.56, the route's only spot: no group). The lone sparkie's `ClusteredNpc` stayed in
  `InstanceWalkerFormations.groupedSpawnObjects`, reachable from the static `WalkerFormationsCache`, and held the first npc for good: its first
  ordinary kill left the corpse alive at refcount 1 with no task until shutdown - the S-1 signature. Its respawn is cached as a new candidate and
  never spawned (Java's own bug, kept). This is the **likeliest** cause, not a proven one, since the log cannot say which npc 25582 was; 125 such
  lone pool >= 2 walker spots exist across the spawn files. Checks for the next session: the sparkie at 1134.95/1012.56 does not come back after
  its first kill (Java does the same), and the census no longer reports its corpse.

What Java reads decides what the port may release: a reference that a Java structure keeps but never reads again is invisible (the garbage
collector would not care either way), so the C++ releases it at the moment Java stops reading it, never earlier; a reference Java keeps reading
stays, and the census then reports a retention Java has too.
