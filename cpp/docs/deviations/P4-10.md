| Area | Java | C++ | Reason |

| `KnownList` pair membership: `addPair` (the pair-add sites of `KnownList.findVisibleObjects`, both loops, and `FlagKnownList.update`), the two-sided removals of `clear` and `forgetObjectsOrUpdateVisibility`, and `FlagKnownList`'s removal | `if (b.getKnownList().add(a)) a.getKnownList().add(b);` and `del(b); b.getKnownList().del(owner);` as independent steps, with no coordination with `World.despawn` or with each other | Every membership change of a pair runs under the pair's striped lock (64 immortal Monitors, lock class `KnownList::pairLocks#stripe`, taken after the owner's `KnownList` monitor and before the `knownObjects` stripes, nothing else locked or called under it): `addPair` inserts both edges, then reads both spawn states (the Dekker handshake with `World.despawn`, which sets `isSpawned(false)` before its clear) and removes both inserts again if either object is despawned or the second insert fails (the owner not aware of the object); the removals take both edges out together. The controller notifications (see, notSee, notKnow) follow after the lock in Java's order; a rolled back insert sends no see, only the removal notifications of `del` | Java race (D6): a pair added concurrently with a despawn, a range removal or another pair add could leave a one-sided edge that no later clear finds (a spawned object keeps a despawned one).
